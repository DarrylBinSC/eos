# O-Insights demo: Copilot AI notes (filed 2026-10-26)

Source: Copilot meeting notes from the O-Insights (CTS) demo, pasted by Darryl 2026-10-26. Shared by Jarrod Booth (Supplier Relations) alongside the vendor's feature comparison workbook (`Deliverables/entroCIM/2026-10-26_O-Insights_Software_Comparison_SOURCE.xlsx`). Demo date not stated; the "mid-July target for readiness" line places it before July 2026. "Jared" in the notes is Jarrod Booth (transcript spelling). Verbatim below; never edited.

---

Platform and deployment: The platform can be cloud-hosted and white-labeled for use under a customer's own brand. The demo focused on a rental-property portfolio with IoT sensor data integrated through MQTT. 0:15

Portfolio dashboards: A map view showed multiple sites with critical-alarm counts and key information. Drilling into a site revealed a three-building layout and dashboards for temperature and leak detection; users could navigate to a building and drill into sensor counts, including out-of-range temperatures and low batteries. Dashboard widgets were described as editable, resizable, and reusable through templates across portfolios. 1:13

Historical insights and playback: The demo showed temperature and occupancy trends across a floor layout. Users could select a date range and playback speed to animate historical readings as changing colors on the layout, then save the playback as a video for review by people without system access. Jared highlighted this feature, and the team said similar time-lapse functionality had previously been introduced in Niagara and XProtect contexts. 4:02

Alarm management and reporting: Operators could filter alarms by status, criticality, assignment, or sensor type; open an alarm to view its recent trend; and acknowledge or hold it. Escalation paths could be configured for unacknowledged alarms, with email, WhatsApp, or SMS mentioned as channels. Scheduled reports included alarm, historical-data, and benchmarking reports; templates could be customized with branding, charts, and reporting parameters. 6:18

Fault detection and diagnosis (FDD): The demo showed a fault dashboard with priority counts, asset breakdowns, and recurring faults. Examples included a sensor value stuck despite related system conditions changing, sensor spikes or drops, and heating continuing while a flat was unoccupied. Operators could view symptoms, possible causes, impacts, and recommendations, and generate fault reports. Rules could be applied across connected assets and sensors. 10:11

FDD configuration: In response to Jared's request, the presenter demonstrated creating an FDD item by naming it, selecting a template and asset or sensor type, then editing it with a visual block workflow. The team described the approach as low-code/no-code, with templates users could modify or create and reuse across sites. 17:31

Conversational AI and interface options: The conversational interface answered plain-language questions about leak counts and active alarms, returned counts and lists, and could display results as a pie chart. A separate help-document assistant provided step-by-step guidance on configuring a workspace widget. The presenter also showed theme and language settings. 13:32

3D visualization: The demo showed a building and floor-plan view with sensors placed in the model, navigation between floors, and the ability to focus on a particular unit or sensor. Existing 3D model files could be converted to supported formats, loaded, rendered, and populated with sensor points. 23:28

Timeline and follow-ups: The team indicated a mid-July target for readiness and said pricing was ready, with pricing information to be shared by email. MQTT was available in the initial release; BACnet, OPC UA, and SNMP were discussed for an end-of-September target. The group also discussed preparing sales collateral and awareness for the sales team. 20:25
