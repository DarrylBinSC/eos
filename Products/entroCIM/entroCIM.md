## RFP and Specification Terminology (added 2026-08-19, Darryl direct)

Recorded during the FRB Cash Services RFP Section 3.1 review. Both facts are durable and will recur as FRB guide-specification adoption spreads across districts (`Products/entroCIM/entroCIM.md`, 08-17 evidence).

**Independent Data Layer (IDL) is an industry term of art, not an entroCIM claim.** It means a data layer that is independent of any single source system, extensible, accessible to the Owner, and serving as the central point of data integration. IDLs are commonly built on SQL, graph, or time-series platforms. A commercially licensed data-and-analytics platform beneath one is normal and is not a contradiction of the word "independent." Do not treat the SkySpark dependency as being in tension with an IDL claim; Claude made that error on 08-19 and Darryl corrected it. The part of the definition that is genuinely testable by an evaluator is **accessible to the Owner**: direct query against a documented schema, whether Owner access needs a separate third-party licence, full export of model/tags/history, and end-of-term data rights. Answer those affirmatively rather than defending the word.

**IDL position confirmed and the three owner-access answers are all yes (Darryl direct, 2026-09-21).** Tested against a live external challenge: Nexus Labs, reviewing the NexusCon 2026 Wesco/Portman CODA session deck, said they would only categorise Wesco as an IDL if Portman could purchase the data layer without purchasing the front-end application, and otherwise call it "a data layer coupled with applications."

Darryl's position, stated directly and not to be softened: **we are an IDL, and we do not accept their definition.** entroCIM provides independence from the vertically integrated controls and applications through an open and available API, licensing that grants that access, and a front end that makes the data usable. Their test is whether the layer can be bought separately, which is a commercial packaging test used as a proxy for an architectural property. It gets the answer wrong in both directions: a closed schema sold as a standalone SKU passes it, and an open, documented, licensed API fails it because the vendor also ships an interface. The question an owner actually needs answered is what happens at end of term, which is whether they keep the model, tags and history and whether another application can read it without re-integration.

**The three testable owner-access criteria from the 08-19 entry are now answered, all affirmative (Darryl, 2026-09-21, "that is part of how we're built"):**

1. API access is included in the standard entroCIM licence. Not a paid add-on, not negotiated per deal.
2. The normalised model, including tags and history, is exportable to the customer at end of term, and that is written down.
3. The API is publicly documented, not documented only to licensed customers.

These are product properties and can be stated externally without further qualification. Use them; they are stronger than arguing the definition.

**Handling guidance where a host or standards body applies the narrower definition.** Do not contest the vocabulary on their platform, which makes Wesco the subject instead of the customer story. Use "vendor-neutral, normalized data layer" in that venue, which is accurate and needs nobody's agreement. Put the actual position on record separately and directly with the body maintaining the definition, framed as input on the definition rather than a complaint about the categorisation, since silent compliance leaves a "coupled" categorisation in their community material. Carried into the NexusCon deck response, 2026-09-21.


**entroCIM is not FIPS 140-2 / 140-3 validated (Darryl, 08-19).** Darryl's assessment is that FIPS validation is not required for FRB, on the evidence that entroCIM is deployed at FRB today. Treat that as the working position. ## LLM access paths and development cost basis (added 2026-08-25, Darryl direct; **corrected same day**)

> This section is about **Wesco's own token consumption while building entroCIM AI**. It is not the customer-facing commercial model recorded above, which says customers supply their own LLM API keys. Both use the phrase "API keys" and mean different things. The customer-supplied-key position is what bounds Wesco's exposure to the development and test period; if it changes, this section understates the consequence badly.

**Two workloads, two access paths, two budgets.**

| Workload | What consumes tokens | Access path | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woolly harness runtime, dev and test | The harness itself calling the LLM | **Direct Anthropic API keys** | **Woolly does not support Azure AI Foundry at this point** (Darryl, 2026-08-25). Technically forced, not a preference |
| Developer coding assistance | Engineers using Claude Code | **Azure AI Foundry** | Claude Code does run on Foundry. The 2026-08-25 evidence records models enabled for the entroCIM and Ascent teams via the `entroCIM_Ops` AD group |

Both satisfy the Wesco AI Council and IT practice requiring **API keys rather than individual accounts** (Darryl, 2026-08-25), which independently forecloses seat-based subscription plans for any Wesco development project.

**Commercial consequence.** Anthropic API spend cannot be absorbed into an existing Azure commitment the way Foundry spend can. It needs its own budget line and its own owner. If Foundry support for Woolly arrives, that separate line goes away.

**Cost basis, Woolly runtime (the Anthropic API keys).** Roughly **$0.21 per Woolly interaction** on Sonnet 5 and **$0.10** on Haiku 4.5, at 2026-08-25 list prices on an assumed profile of 4 agentic turns and 148K input tokens per interaction. For six months of development and test at 280 interactions per active day: **$4,030 expected, $6,050 as a budget line, range $2,015 to $12,090.** Recommended Console workspace spend limit $1,000 per month.

**Cost basis, developer assistance (Azure Foundry).** Roughly **$195 per developer per month** on a Sonnet-primary mix, or $290 with contingency. For 5 developers over 6 months: $5,840 expected. This figure reconciles to Anthropic's published enterprise benchmarks within 17%.

**Confidence differs sharply between the two, and it matters.** The developer-assistance figure can be validated against published enterprise data. The Woolly figure cannot: no published benchmark exists for an embedded agent harness, and no Wesco measurement has been taken. Every input in that model is a reasoned assumption. **Quote it as a range, not a point.** Instrumenting roughly twenty real Woolly interactions on the dev server would replace every assumption with a measured figure; Ron Snyder owns that environment and it is half a day of work.

**The finding worth carrying.** In the harness workload, cost is driven by **context size and interaction volume, not developer headcount**. Adding an engineer barely moves it; enlarging the test site model moves it a great deal. The automated regression suite is 56% of modelled volume and is the only line with no human deciding each call, which makes it the runaway risk. Three controls in order of value: mock the LLM response for tests exercising plumbing rather than model quality, run remaining automated traffic on Haiku, and set the workspace spend limit before the suite is automated.

## Support Services SLA — document located 2026-08-26

`Products/entroCIM/Support SLA/entroCIM SME Support Services SLA.docx`. **Authored by Darryl, dated 2026-02-22.** It existed for six months before this record held it; the support-model thread in `03` and `04` was tracking a gap that was partly already closed, because the document lived outside the folder. Correct the framing rather than repeat it: what was undefined was the product-support to Managed Services *interface*, not the customer-facing SLA.

**Structure.** Two instruments that work together, which is the point: **SME hours** are a finite contracted pool that funds the work; the **SLA** sets priority and response targets for how that work is handled. The document title, "SME Support Services SLA," carries the combination deliberately.

**In scope:** entroCIM product support (updates, revisions, functionality guidance and troubleshooting) and support of what Wesco already delivered in the deployment scope, being existing integrations and point mappings, graphics, alarms and notifications, KPIs and dashboards, and rules, logic and standard analytics previously configured by Wesco.

**Out of scope, requiring SOW or change order:** anything adding net-new capability, coverage, assets, devices, integrations, graphics packages, KPIs, alarm strategy expansion, analytics or rules, or substantial redesign beyond minor correction and tuning.

**Coverage:** Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 17:00 US Eastern, excluding Wesco holidays, unless otherwise contracted.

**Priorities.** Response is acknowledgement and triage start; restoration is a best-effort goal, not a guarantee.

| Priority | Response | Restoration (best effort) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Critical: platform down, core functions unavailable, critical alarms broadly not functioning | 1 business hour | 1 business day |
| P2 High: major degradation, key functions impaired, limited workaround | 4 business hours | 3 business days |
| P3 Medium: partial loss of non-critical function, workaround exists | 1 business day | 10 business days |
| P4 Low or request: how-to, minor defects, cosmetic issues in existing scope | 2 business days | Scheduled as capacity allows |

**Commercial guardrail.** SLA targets apply only to in-scope requests and only while contracted SME hours remain. Timers pause while awaiting customer access, approvals or information. Out-of-scope work stops and an SOW is proposed. Exhausted hours can pause support until more are purchased.

**Reporting is marked "recommended," not required.** Monthly ticket volume by priority, response performance, and SME hours consumed against remaining. **Two consequences worth acting on:** recommended reporting is the kind that does not happen, and this is the same response-performance data the entroCIM platform availability metric in the FY27 product deck needs. Making it required serves both.

**Live relevance 2026-08-26:** the JCI Airwall MSP review records Wesco L1/L2 obligations as undefined against the entroCIM product-support model. This document is the answer to part of that, and the contract review with Brian Miller is Friday 2026-08-28.
