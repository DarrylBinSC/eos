**2026-07-28 — Airwall Airspan port mirroring vs. Nozomi Arc (feeds queue item 6, sensor placement for the field node).** Question: why Arc failed behind an Airwall port mirror at Darryl's prior company. Method: Tempered/Airwall webhelp + Nozomi public docs. Findings: (a) Airspan (v2.2.11+, internal process "Aircap") mirrors only traffic crossing the mirror-source gateway's tunnels; local device-to-device and link-local discovery traffic on the protected segment never enters the mirror. (b) Mirrored traffic is delivered GRE/ERSPAN-encapsulated over its own Airwall tunnel; Tempered's documented Nozomi target is Guardian with ERSPAN Type II. No public evidence Arc decapsulates ERSPAN/GRE; Arc is a host sensor doing raw local-NIC capture and subnet sensing. (c) Airwall multicast forwarding is naive (flood to all peers, no IGMP/PIM), overlay port groups have a setting that suppresses broadcast/multicast, L2/non-IP and TTL=1 link-local multicast doesn't cross the L3 overlay. (d) Tempered warns of 3–5x performance penalty and packet fragmentation on mirrored traffic. Assessment (inference): the pairing was wrong, not broken — Airspan was engineered for Guardian; Arc was never the intended mirror consumer. Design implication for CDA field node: put the network sensor (Guardian or containerized equivalent) on the protected-segment side of the Airwall, local to the devices; use Arc for host coverage; do not architect mirror-through-tunnel as the visibility path. Verify with Nozomi: Arc ERSPAN support roadmap, container licensing. Confidence: high on documented mechanics, medium on root-cause attribution to the prior-company incident (config unknown). Sources: repo.tempered.io webhelp (int_packet_analyzers, port_mirror_definitions, kb_aw_multicast), nozominetworks.com Arc pages.
