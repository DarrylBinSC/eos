## Slide 1 — Why now

**Title (the message):** "The devices we sell keep creating value we do not capture"

Statements:
- Connected devices, cameras, access control, network equipment, are installed and then left behind: firmware unpatched, health unknown until failure, cyber risk accumulating, no accountable owner.
- Customers do not want another tool to staff. They want someone accountable.
- CSS needs recurring service revenue and stronger installed-base monetization. This problem is the opening.

Speaker notes: qualitative on financials; the mid-year software and pull-thru picture is context Sean already knows, reference verbally, not on the slide. The market has validated the pain: OEM tools and specialist platforms exist, but they are single-vendor or customer-operated.

## Value-proposition talk track (added by Darryl, 07-27 evening)

Use verbally on the offer/platform slides:
- **A single platform for managing device firmware** — replaces the per-manufacturer tool sprawl (each OEM's free utility, separately staffed, separately ignored).
- **One platform and one interface for your Wesco firmware** — the devices you buy through Wesco come with one place to see, validate, and manage them. Ties the service directly to the distribution relationship.

This is the consolidation argument: the answer to "the OEM tools are free" is that five free tools nobody operates are more expensive than one interface someone is accountable for.

## Slide 2 — What the customer buys

**Title:** "Connected Device Assurance: covered devices, evidence, and a service level"

Structure: two tiers, drawn as a simple ladder.
- **Base: Assure.** Cyber-hygiene visibility from a partner sensor (endpoint and OT asset discovery, vulnerability context) plus Wesco device status and health: functional checks, diagnostics, validation, alerting, field escalation, service evidence. One interface, delivered by Wesco.
- **Advanced: Manage.** Firmware and configuration management for eligible devices: verified product models, versions validated through direct device integration, controlled updates with customer approval and evidence, maintenance scheduling, lifecycle planning.

Data-fidelity line beneath the ladder: **discovered (partner sensor) → validated (device integration) → managed (controlled action)**.

Speaker notes: remediation and controllability vary by product line, which is why the tier split matters; the customer buys an outcome and a service level, not software.
