## Investment Description

**High-level value statement:**

Connected Device Assurance Services addresses a growing and largely unmanaged cyber risk: the connected devices on customer sites. Cameras, access control, and network equipment are deployed and then left under-managed or unmanaged: inventories incomplete, firmware out of date, health unknown until failure, and cyber posture degrading device by device, with no accountable owner.

The service takes ownership of that risk end to end:

- **Identify the risk.** Covered devices are inventoried and assessed for firmware currency, known vulnerabilities, support status, and configuration exposure. Automated discovery accelerates this: devices are found and profiled without a manual audit, so coverage is reached quickly and maintained as the estate changes.
- **Monitor the risk.** Posture and device health are tracked continuously, not sampled in a periodic assessment. New vendor advisories are matched against the covered estate as they are published, and changes in device condition are surfaced as prioritized work rather than raw alerts.
- **Manage the upgrades.** Wesco maintains the approved firmware and configuration baseline per device family, checks compatibility and prerequisites, plans the change, and obtains customer approval and a maintenance window before anything is touched.
- **Execute the upgrades.** Wesco or a Wesco partner performs the update remotely through the service platform: staged rollout, post-change validation, supported rollback or a defined recovery path if a device fails, and evidence of every step. Where remote resolution is not possible, the work escalates to field service with a complete work package.
- **Who does the work.** Wesco Managed Services owns the service and the service level, delivering directly or through qualified partners under Wesco's process, approvals, and evidence standards. Accountability stays with Wesco in either case. The customer approves and receives evidence; they do not staff a tool, run the updates, or absorb the coordination.
- **Why Wesco.** We sell and distribute these devices, hold the supplier relationships behind them, already operate the platforms this service is built on, and can carry a device from a detected risk through remediation to replacement and installation. No point tool or single-manufacturer utility spans that path.

**It is also the lead-in service for the CSS digital portfolio.** The secure connection and verified device inventory it establishes are what Intelligent Asset Management Services (WAM integration) and entroCIM-enabled services such as Intelligent Facility Management Services need to begin. Customers can start with a contained cyber engagement and expand without a second onboarding.

**Structured value proposition (template format):**

- **For** security, facility, and IT/OT operations leaders accountable for cyber risk in connected-device estates
- **who** face growing exposure from under-managed and unmanaged devices: out-of-date firmware, unpatched vulnerabilities, degrading security posture, incomplete inventory, and device health discovered only at failure
- **the** Connected Device Assurance Services offering
- **is a** recurring managed service that keeps device firmware, configuration, health, and security posture current through one platform and one interface, on site or delivered from the Wesco cloud, with automated discovery to establish and maintain coverage
- **that** measurably reduces device cyber risk and downtime: continuous posture visibility, customer-approved remediation, validated outcomes, audit-ready evidence, and planned lifecycle and refresh recommendations
- **unlike** per-manufacturer utilities the customer must staff, periodic assessments that age the day they are delivered, or point tools that stop at visibility
- **our solution** puts an accountable service behind the risk: one interface across manufacturers, remediation rather than reporting alone, device condition connected directly to replacement and field service through Wesco's distribution relationships, and a foundation that extends into Wesco's asset management and facility services without a second onboarding.

## Nonfunctional Requirements

**Paste-ready text.**

- Security: least-privilege access to customer devices; segregated credential handling with no shared or embedded credentials; customer-approved connectivity; alignment with Wesco cyber governance and customer security review
- Change control: no action on a customer device without recorded authorization, an approved window, and pre-change validation
- Auditability: immutable evidence record per action, retained to a defined period, exportable to the customer
- Availability and performance: service platform availability appropriate to a business-hours managed service, with defined behavior on loss of connectivity, including local buffering and safe resumption
- Scalability: support growth from pilot estate to multi-site, multi-customer production without per-customer custom engineering
- Multi-tenancy: strict tenant isolation of device data, evidence, and credentials
- Data handling: customer data segregated by tenant with defined retention. Used to deliver the service and, under the customer agreement, to enable connected Wesco services such as WAM, entroCIM services, and Navigator. Not sold; no disclosure outside the service delivery chain without customer permission
- Supportability: defined tiered support model with escalation to engineering, manufacturer, or partner
- Compliance alignment: capability set mapped to recognized device-security guidance (NIST and CISA references), without claiming certification

## Out of Scope

*Accuracy notes (not for submission), important for how these are cited:*
1. *CISA OT asset inventory guidance is the strongest reference for this service. It is aimed squarely at device owners and operators, it is recent and multi-agency, and its core premise, that organizations cannot protect what they cannot see, maps directly to the discovery and inventory capability. Lead with it.*
2. *NIST IR 8259A is written for device manufacturers, not operators. Cite it as the definition of what a securable device must support, which is what the service maintains. Do not imply it places an obligation on our customers.*
3. *EU CRA obligations fall on manufacturers of products with digital elements, not on device owners. For US customers it is context and direction of travel, not a compliance driver. Frame it as evidence that device security expectations are hardening across the industry, and only claim direct applicability where a customer sells into the EU. Overstating this is the kind of claim a reviewer can check.*
