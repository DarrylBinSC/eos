## Investment Description:

Connected Device Assurance Services addresses a growing and largely unmanaged cyber risk: the connected devices on customer sites. Cameras, access control, and network equipment are deployed and then left under-managed or unmanaged: inventories incomplete, firmware out of date, health unknown until failure, and cyber posture degrading device by device, with no accountable owner.

The service takes ownership of that risk end to end:

Establish the compliance framework. Wesco helps the customer stand up the operating process device compliance depends on: intake and registration of new devices, classification and risk review, approval and deployment standards, exception handling, and retirement policy. The customer owns the policy and the accountability; Wesco supplies the design, the workflow, and the evidence that keeps it running.

Identify the risk. Covered devices are inventoried and assessed for firmware currency, known vulnerabilities, support status, and configuration exposure. Automated discovery accelerates this: devices are found and profiled without a manual audit, so coverage is reached quickly and maintained as the envrionment changes.

Monitor the risk. Posture and device health are tracked continuously, not sampled in a periodic assessment. New vendor advisories are matched against the covered devices as they are published, and changes in device condition are surfaced as prioritized work rather than raw alerts.

Manage the upgrades. Wesco maintains the approved firmware and configuration baseline per device family, checks compatibility and prerequisites, plans the change, and obtains customer approval and a maintenance window before anything is touched.

Execute the upgrades. Wesco or a Wesco partner performs the update remotely through the service platform: staged rollout, post-change validation, supported rollback or a defined recovery path if a device fails, and evidence of every step. Where remote resolution is not possible, the work escalates to field service with a complete work package.

Who does the work. Wesco Managed Services owns the service and the service level. Wesco TSS professional services deliver the compliance framework design and onboarding. Delivery may be direct or through qualified partners under Wesco's process, approvals, and evidence standards, with accountability retained by Wesco. The customer approves and receives evidence; they do not staff a tool, run the updates, or absorb the coordination.

Why Wesco. Industry guidance is clear that tools accelerate compliance but cannot substitute for governance. Wesco supplies both: the operating process, the platform that executes it, and the managed service that sustains it, on devices we sell and support. We hold the supplier relationships behind those devices, already operate the platforms this service is built on, and can carry a device from a detected risk through remediation to replacement and installation. No point tool or single-manufacturer utility spans that path.

Lead-in service for the CSS digital portfolio: The secure connection and verified device inventory it establishes are what Intelligent Asset Management Services (WAM integration) and entroCIM-enabled services such as Intelligent Facility Management Services need to begin. Customers can start with a contained cyber engagement and expand without a second onboarding.

For security, facility, and IT/OT operations leaders accountable for cyber risk in connected-device footprint

who face growing exposure from under-managed and unmanaged devices: out-of-date firmware, unpatched vulnerabilities, degrading security posture, incomplete inventory, and device health discovered only at failure

the Connected Device Assurance Services offering

is a recurring managed service that keeps device firmware, configuration, health, and security posture current through one platform and one interface, on site or delivered from the Wesco cloud, with automated discovery to establish and maintain coverage

that measurably reduces device cyber risk and downtime: continuous posture visibility, customer-approved remediation, validated outcomes, audit-ready evidence, and planned lifecycle and refresh recommendations

unlike per-manufacturer utilities the customer must staff, periodic assessments that age the day they are delivered, or point tools that stop at visibility

our solution puts an accountable service behind the risk: one interface across manufacturers, remediation rather than reporting alone, device condition connected directly to replacement and field service through Wesco's system integrator relationships, and a foundation that extends into Wesco's asset management and facility services without a second onboarding.

## What are the risks of NOT doing this?

Without this investment, the connected devices Wesco sells remain unmanaged after the sale. Customer cyber exposure continues to grow with no Wesco answer, the recurring revenue and refresh demand attached to the installed base go to specialist providers, and the platform investments already made stay confined to a single service.

Growing customer cyber exposure with no Wesco answer Unmanaged device firmware, configuration, and credentials are an expanding cyber and compliance liability for customers, and demand for accountable remediation is rising across OT and IoT environments. Customers will address this need with or without Wesco. Without an offer, they build the trusted device relationship with someone else, and Wesco is left supplying hardware into an envrionment another provider manages.

Ceding the category to specialist providers Third-party device-management platforms already sell recurring device-security services into the customer segments Wesco serves. Each year of delay strengthens their position, raises the cost of eventual entry, and narrows the differentiation available to Wesco.

Unrealized recurring revenue from devices already sold Cameras, access control, and network devices require firmware, configuration, health, and security upkeep for their entire operating life. Wesco sells the devices but does not participate in that recurring need today. The subscription revenue attached to the installed base is left unserved or captured by others.

Lost refresh and pull-through demand An assurance service identifies end-of-support conditions, repeated failures, and replacement candidates from its own operating data and delivers them to Sales as qualified opportunities. Without it, refresh demand stays invisible until a competitor surfaces it.

No lead-in service for the digital portfolio Connected Device Assurance establishes the secure connection and verified device inventory that Intelligent Asset Management Services and Intelligent Facility Management enabled services need to begin. Without it, each service must establish its own device connection and inventory, raising delivery cost across the portfolio and removing the low-friction entry point that makes expansion easier to sell.

Stranded return-on-platform investment already made The service is assembled from capabilities Wesco has already funded: entroCIM and its containerization work, WAM lifecycle data, and secure remote connectivity. A focused investment converts these into a revenue-bearing service. Not proceeding leaves that reuse unrealized.

## Nonfunctional Reqs. (e.g. Security, Performance, etc.)

Security: least-privilege access to customer devices; segregated credential handling with no shared or embedded credentials; customer-approved connectivity; alignment with Wesco cyber governance and customer security review

Change control: no action on a customer device without recorded authorization, an approved window, and pre-change validation

Auditability: immutable evidence record per action, retained to a defined period, exportable to the customer

Availability and performance: service platform availability appropriate to a business-hours managed service, with defined behavior on loss of connectivity, including local buffering and safe resumption

Scalability: support growth from pilot footprint to multi-site, multi-customer production without per-customer custom engineering

Multi-tenancy: strict tenant isolation of device data, evidence, and credentials

Data handling: customer data segregated by tenant with defined retention. Used to deliver the service and, under the customer agreement, to enable connected Wesco services such as WAM, entroCIM services, and Navigator. Not sold; no disclosure outside the service delivery chain without customer permission

Supportability: defined tiered support model with escalation to engineering, manufacturer, or partner

Compliance alignment: capability set mapped to recognized device-security guidance (NIST and CISA references), without claiming certification

## Links: (Other supporting documents, feasibility or trade studies, models, market analysis, etc.)

External guidance and standards

CISA, Foundations for OT Cybersecurity: Asset Inventory Guidance for Owners and Operators (August 2025). https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/foundations-ot-cybersecurity-asset-inventory-guidance-owners-and-operators

What it is: joint guidance from CISA, NSA, FBI, EPA and five international agencies establishing device asset inventory as a foundational requirement for owners and operators of connected and OT environments.

Why it matters: it makes device inventory an expectation rather than a best practice, and its core premise, that organizations cannot protect what they cannot see, is the problem this service solves first.

CISA, Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals, Version 2.0 (December 2025). https://www.cisa.gov/cross-sector-cybersecurity-performance-goals

What it is: CISA's baseline set of cybersecurity practices for critical infrastructure operators, covering device security, vulnerability management, and governance.

Why it matters: it defines the ongoing practices customers are expected to sustain, which is what a managed service delivers and a one-time assessment does not.

NIST IR 8259A, IoT Device Cybersecurity Capability Core Baseline. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2020/NIST.IR.8259A.pdf

What it is: the NIST baseline defining the capabilities a connected device must support to be securable, including device identification, secure software update, configuration control, and state awareness.

Why it matters: it defines the technical standard the service maintains on each covered device. Note that it is written for device manufacturers, so it is cited as the definition of a securable device, not as an obligation on customers.

EU Cyber Resilience Act. https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act

What it is: EU regulation on products with digital elements. Vulnerability and incident reporting obligations apply from September 2026, with full application from December 2027.

Why it matters: it is evidence that device security expectations are hardening across the industry. The obligations fall on manufacturers rather than device owners, so it is cited as direction of travel and applies directly only to customers selling into the EU.

Internal references

Operationalizing IoT Compliance and Cybersecurity for Enterprise Networks. Internal white paper, commissioned by SPARC/VOI and authored by Wesco.

What it is: a vendor-neutral framework for SPARC/VOI enterprise members covering IoT compliance process design, what device-security tools do and do not solve, and operating models from low-cost foundations through partner-led managed delivery.

Why it matters: it documents demand from the enterprise buyer community itself. It concludes that tools cannot substitute for governance and identifies partner-led managed delivery as the fastest path to operational maturity, which is the model this investment proposes.

Connected Device Assurance solution analysis.

What it is: the internal service definition, architecture, competitive review, manufacturer qualification detail, pilot design, and dependency assessment behind this business case.

Why it matters: it is the evidence base for the scope, deployment model, and pilot approach proposed here.

Manufacturer technical documentation for the launch device families.

What it is: vendor documentation confirming supported firmware management interfaces, update mechanisms, validation, and rollback behavior.

Why it matters: it establishes that controlled remote firmware management is technically supported on the devices selected for the pilot.

Executive review materials presented to CSS leadership, July 2026.

What it is: the service concept, architecture, portfolio fit, and funding path reviewed with CSS leadership.

Why it matters: it records the leadership endorsement to proceed to the AOP submission.
