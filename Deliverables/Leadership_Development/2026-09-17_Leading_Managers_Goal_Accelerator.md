## 1. Overall goal

*What goal would you like to set that will impact your career success or development? Achievable within the next six months.*

By mid-March 2027, every product owner in my organization owns their product end to end, from technical requirements through to how the product is explained to sales, customers and leadership, and I am no longer covering either half for anyone.

The development is mine as much as theirs. My delegation strength is with teams; with individuals I fill gaps rather than close them. This goal forces the individual version of the skill.

---

## 2. The Motivation

*Choose two questions. Answer the ones that feel authentic.*

**Why is this important?**

Product ownership is the load-bearing role in my portfolio. A product owner who only carries the technical half produces work that sales and customers cannot use; one who only carries the communication half commits us to things engineering did not scope. I have been absorbing the difference personally, which keeps delivery moving and keeps the role half-learned. It also puts me in work that is below the level I am paid to work at, so the cost lands twice.

**What might happen if I don't develop this?**

The portfolio has outgrown what I can personally backstop. [assumed] If I keep gap-filling, three things follow: my product owners plateau in a partially defined role, my attention stays in execution rather than strategy and executive alignment, and the organization's capacity stays capped at my availability. None of those show up as a failure in any given week, which is exactly why it persists.

## 3. Activities and Plan

*What do you need to do or change to achieve your goal? How will you know when you have achieved it?*

**What I need to change:** stop treating gap-filling as delivery insurance. Three specific changes. Write down what full product ownership means in my group, because it has never been stated and my covering has been the working definition. Agree with each product owner where they sit against it and which half they need to build. Then hold the line when a deadline makes it cheaper for me to just do it, which is the moment the whole thing actually turns on.

**How I will know:** I am not authoring or rewriting material that belongs to a product owner; each of them presents their own product to leadership and to sales without me in front of it; and each has a development objective against their weaker half that they are measurably working on. The honest test is the one I have used my whole career: what gets measured gets done, so I am measuring my own gap-filling, not just their progress.

## 5. Challenge Management

**Which key activities are at risk of roadblocks?**

Two. Handing each product owner a named forum, because the forum happens whether or not they are ready and the first weak showing creates pressure on me to take it back. And holding the line during an escalation or a planning cycle, when the cost of coaching is paid in visible delay and the cost of covering is invisible.

**What challenges might throw me off my game?**

Experienced people who read past success as evidence they do not need to change, and who resist teaming, thinking differently, or admitting when something went wrong. Expect resistance to be framed as efficiency rather than as refusal.

Capacity is the second one. If a product owner is genuinely underwater, "own the full role" is a demand rather than a development opportunity, and the plan has to distinguish between will and bandwidth. `[assumed: PO capacity is a live constraint, based on it being an open item in your own register]`

The third is me. Under deadline pressure my reflex is to route around resistance rather than through it, and every instance of that is invisible to everyone except the person whose development just stopped.

**How will I respond and get back on track?**

Decide the rule before the pressure arrives, not during it: when something is late or wrong, the first move is a coaching conversation with the owner, not a rescue. If I take work back, it is named out loud as a temporary exception with a date it returns, so nobody has to guess whether the role changed.

Track my own takeovers monthly and treat a rising count as the signal, not their output. Separate the two resistance cases early: unwillingness gets named directly in a one-on-one; capacity gets fixed with scope or support, because coaching does not solve arithmetic.
