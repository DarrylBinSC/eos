## Prompt

Marquet is recognized for implementing radical delegation, which he achieved through giving control to his crew by building mastery and providing clarity. What is one way you could give up a tiny bit of control while helping your team build mastery and providing clarity?

## Reply (draft)

The control I need to give up is gap-filling.

My product owners tend to be strong on one side of the role, either the technical requirements or the communication, and I have been quietly covering the other side. It gets the work out and it keeps the role half-learned.

The small version: the next stakeholder update goes out under the product owner's name. I review it, I do not write it.

For that to be fair, clarity comes first. Product ownership in my group runs from technical requirements through to how the product is explained to sales, customers and leadership. If I have never said that plainly, then my gap-filling has been the definition of the role, and the gap is mine.

Mastery comes from doing the unfamiliar half with support, not from watching me do it. It also shows me where someone genuinely needs development, which I cannot see while I am covering for them.

They get the whole job. I get my attention back for work only I can do.
