**Subject:** i3 Alliance Agreement — Section 12, my comments

Brandon,

With the Section 8 edits accepted, I have gone through Section 12. This is my last pass on this document and it is deliberately narrow: I have looked at it from the Digital Products side only, which means the one question of whether Section 12 limits our ability to work with other partners on data center development where our contacts and i3's overlap. The remaining legal elements are yours and I am content to leave them there.

Short answer. 12.2 and 12.3, the clauses that look like the constraint, are close to workable. 12.4 is the constraint, and it is the one item in this section I would not sign as drafted. It sits inside a non-circumvention clause but is not project-scoped, not time-limited, applies to any third party, and attaches to the Section 6.2 Core IP definition, which claims the graph models, ontologies and schemas layer our own platform occupies.

## Changes I am asking for, in priority order

| # | Clause | What it does to us | Change requested |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12.4 Anti-replication | Not project-scoped, not time-limited, applies to "any third party," and perpetual through Section 16.6. Combined with the Section 6.2 Core IP definition it constrains what we can build and deliver with any other data center partner, permanently, against a standard i3 defines and never has to prove. Our platform sits on that layer today. | Burden on i3 to identify the specific Core IP actually used. Exclude methods that are generally known, industry-standard, or open-source. Exclude residual knowledge held in our engineers' unaided memory. Limit to two years after termination. |
| 2 | 12.2(b) Displacement | No knowledge or intent qualifier and no exception for a client-initiated change. If a developer decides on its own to change integrators, or asks us to bring in a different partner, or if we team with someone else for scope i3 does not hold, we are exposed. | Change to "knowingly induce," and exclude a client's independently initiated decision to change or add providers. |
| 3 | New 12.6 Freedom to partner | 12.1 says the section does not restrict general market access, but that is a statement of intent, not an operative term. Nothing in the section affirmatively preserves our right to team with others, including i3 competitors. | Add a short subsection confirming we may team with, subcontract to, or refer business to any party on any project, including an i3 Project for scope i3 does not perform. |
| 4 | 12.2(a) Sourcing qualifier | "That i3 introduced or sourced" reads onto only the second half of the sentence, so a client we brought ourselves can be caught for the same scope. | Make the sourcing qualifier govern the whole subsection. |
| 5 | 12.2 Undefined terms | "i3 Project," "involvement... ends," and "directly related" are undefined. On a phased campus, i3 can argue a later phase or an adjacent site is directly related and that our involvement runs to commissioning, which stretches a six-month tail into years. | Tie "i3 Project" to a signed project-specific agreement, define a Completion Date, and limit "directly related" to the same facility or site and the awarded scope. |
| 6 | 12.3 Product sales | Our product and services sales are protected only when "made independently of any i3 Project," which reintroduces risk on the one project where we are actually selling material. | Exclude product, equipment and supply chain sales outright. |

Proposed language for each of these is in the attached memo.
