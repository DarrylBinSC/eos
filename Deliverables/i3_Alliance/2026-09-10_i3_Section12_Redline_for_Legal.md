**Scope:** Digital Products review only, and final. Tests one question: does Section 12 restrict Wesco's freedom to work with other partners on data center development opportunities where contacts overlap with i3. Legal-drafting items are listed in Part B and left to Legal's judgment.

## Position

Sections 12.2 and 12.3 are close to workable. They are project-scoped, carry a six-month tail, and protect pre-existing and independently sourced relationships. Four defects in 12.2 still reach Wesco-sourced clients and client-initiated provider changes.

Section 12.4 is the material issue and the only item here that should not be signed as drafted. It is not project-scoped, not time-limited, perpetual through Section 16.6, applies to any third party, and attaches to a Section 6.2 Core IP definition claiming the graph models, ontologies and schemas layer entroCIM occupies through SkySpark and Haystack tagging. Section 15.2 removes the liability cap for IP claims, so the exposure is uncapped as well as undefined.

# Part A — Items requested

## Item 1 — 12.4 Anti-Replication of i3 IP (replacement). Priority 1

**Issue.** Four compounding defects. "Substantially similar methodology derived from i3's Core IP" is undefined and unbounded, and Section 6.2 defines Core IP to include graph models, ontologies and schemas. The restriction is perpetual by operation of Section 16.6. It applies to any third party, which makes it a restraint on Wesco's other partnerships rather than a protection of i3's methodology. The independent-development sentence places no burden on i3 and does not address residual knowledge held by Wesco engineers who complete i3 certification.

**Proposed replacement.**

> 12.4 Anti-Replication of i3 IP. Independent of any project, the Partner shall not, for a period of two (2) years following termination of this Agreement, knowingly replicate, adapt, or commercially deploy the Energy Architecture – Integration, the i3 Portfolio, or the IDRP framework, in each case as documented in writing and provided to the Partner by i3, in competition with i3 or for any third party, without i3's prior written consent. For purposes of this Section 12.4, i3 Core IP does not include: (a) methods, models, schemas, ontologies, tagging conventions, or data structures that are generally known, publicly available, industry-standard, or derived from open standards or open-source frameworks; (b) material the Partner developed independently or held prior to disclosure by i3; or (c) information retained in the unaided memory of the Partner's personnel. Nothing in this Section prohibits the Partner from independently developing any solution without use of i3 Core IP, regardless of whether that solution shares similarities with i3 IP. i3 bears the burden of demonstrating actual use of specifically identified i3 Core IP.

**Rationale.** Converts an open-ended restraint into a defined, provable, time-limited protection of documented methodology. The three exclusions are what prevent the clause reaching entroCIM's own modeling layer. The burden-of-proof sentence is the single most important addition: without it, i3 asserts and Wesco disproves.

**Fallback if i3 refuses the two-year term.** Accept perpetuity only if the exclusions and the burden of proof stand in full and the Section 15.2 IP carve-out from the liability cap is removed.

## Item 2 — 12.2(b) Displacement (amendment). Priority 2

**Issue.** "Act to displace or replace i3 as a provider" carries no knowledge or intent qualifier and no exception for a client-initiated change or for teaming on scope i3 does not hold. As drafted, a developer's own decision to change providers is Wesco's exposure.

**Proposed change.** Replace "act to displace or replace i3 as a provider on that i3 Project" with "knowingly induce that client or counterparty to displace or replace i3 as a provider on that i3 Project," and add to the closing sentence of 12.2 an express exclusion for "a client's independently initiated decision to change or add providers."

**Rationale.** Preserves i3's protection against actual circumvention. Removes exposure for outcomes Wesco does not control.

---

## Item 3 — New 12.6 Freedom to Partner (addition). Priority 3

**Issue.** Nothing in Section 12 affirmatively preserves Wesco's right to work with other parties, including i3 competitors, on the same opportunity or on an i3 Project for scope i3 does not perform. Section 12.1 disclaims restriction on "general access to the market," which is a statement of intent rather than an operative term.

**Proposed new subsection.**

> 12.6 Freedom to Partner. Nothing in this Agreement restricts the Partner from working with, teaming with, subcontracting to, referring business to, or receiving referrals from any other person or entity, including competitors of i3, on any project, including an i3 Project with respect to scope not performed by i3. The Parties acknowledge that the Partner maintains independent relationships across the market and may hold obligations to other partners on a given opportunity. The Partner is under no obligation to route any opportunity to i3, and the Partner's election to pursue an opportunity with another party is not a breach of this Section 12.

**Rationale.** Converts 12.1's stated principle into an operative term. This is the clause to point to when a client relationship is shared and Wesco elects to tie the business to a different partner.

---

## Item 4 — 12.2(a) Sourcing qualifier (amendment). Priority 4

**Issue.** "That i3 introduced or sourced" reads as modifying only "opportunities directly related to it," leaving the scope-of-project restriction applicable to clients Wesco itself sourced.

**Proposed change.** Recast 12.2(a) as: "bypass i3 to solicit, pursue, or contract directly with a client or counterparty that i3 introduced or sourced to the Partner, for the awarded scope of that i3 Project or for a directly related opportunity at the same facility or site."

**Rationale.** A partner who takes an i3-sourced client and cuts i3 out of the awarded scope is still caught. A client Wesco brought to the table is not.

---

## Item 5 — 12.2 Definitions and carve-outs (amendment). Priority 5

**Issue.** "i3 Project," "the Partner's involvement in that i3 Project ends," and "directly related" are undefined, so the six-month tail has no fixed start and the subject matter has no fixed boundary. An unconsummated joint pursuit could also be characterized after the fact as an i3 Project. There is no exception for competitive procurement.

**Proposed changes.** Define "i3 Project" as a project undertaken "under a written project-specific agreement or statement of work signed by both Parties." Add: "The 'Completion Date' is the earliest of completion of the Partner's scope on the i3 Project, termination of the Partner's involvement, or written notice from either Party that the Partner's involvement has ended," and run the six-month tail from that date. Add to the closing sentence of 12.2 exclusions for "the client's other facilities, sites, phases, business units, or affiliates," "subsequent phases or expansions outside the awarded scope," and "responses to a general solicitation, public procurement, request for proposal, or competitive bid."

**Rationale.** Fixes the start date and the subject matter of the restriction. Without this, a phased campus can be argued into a multi-year restraint.

---

## Item 6 — 12.3 Product and services sales (amendment). Priority 6

**Issue.** The existing carve-out protects "Partner's product and services sales made independently of any i3 Project." The qualifier reintroduces risk on the one project where Wesco is actually selling material, which is the ordinary case for a distributor.

**Proposed change.** Strike "made independently of any i3 Project" from the second sentence of 12.3, and add to the closing sentence of 12.2 an exclusion for "the Partner's sale of products, materials, equipment, or supply chain services to any party."

**Rationale.** Distribution is Wesco's core business and is not a circumvention risk in any form this section is aimed at.
