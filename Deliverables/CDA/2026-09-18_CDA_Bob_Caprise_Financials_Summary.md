## Bottom line

Bob gave CDA three things it did not have: a method for the financial case (market sizing down to a customer-level pro forma), a candidate funding mechanism (a per-camera fee on Axis hardware that Kroger has already signaled it would pay for), and a sponsor path (Bill Muczko, framed around Bill's own goal of moving the sales force from pick-pack-ship to advanced services). He also added a second anchor opportunity, Cisco's own real estate, which reads more as an entroCIM energy play than a CDA play and needs a routing decision.

## What Bob said, by theme

**1. How to build the financial case (Wesco sales-plan method).**
Start macro and narrow: total addressable market, then the portion Wesco chooses to address, then strategy and execution, and only then the pro forma. Build the pro forma bottom-up from one or two real customer environments using per-site device densities Wesco already has (his illustrative figures: ~140 cameras per store, ~40 Wi-Fi access points, ~14 intelligent UPSs across IDF/MDF). Enterprise pricing is similar enough across brands that round assumptions will not be materially wrong. His rule: "Every idea starts with a pro forma," and nothing gets rubber on the road until there is a funding source. Precedents he cited: Room-in-a-Box launched on a Microsoft award; the connected-devices cellular play launched on a UBS-funded customer.

**2. Scope beyond cameras.**
Design for the full connected-device estate, not only what Wesco sold (cameras, APs, UPSs, PDUs, POS). Echoes Brad Stevens. His caution: a narrow single-system sale (the Disney sound-masking example) is a band-aid, not a platform, and does not grow. Go where the device count and the customer's pain are, not where Wesco happens to have a foothold.

**3. Anchor customer and funding mechanism: Kroger.**
Kroger has buying signals: it has wanted Axis Device Manager, roughly $15 per camera. Bob had been planning, for 2027, to add a few dollars (he said "five or six") to the Axis camera price and return value under a rebate-type model. His suggestion: instead of the Axis tool, deliver a Wesco-branded (not proprietary) capability, and that per-camera fee becomes CDA's funding model. Darryl's framing in response: an upfront fee for device and software, plus an ongoing service fee, with the software cost carried inside the service value (reporting, fault mitigation, remediation). Darryl also noted a TSS resource already performing hands-on service for Kroger.

**4. Reference model: Axis + Cisco Meraki.**
Bob described the Axis–Meraki two-subscription model as "exactly what you're building": a device-health subscription (downtime, telemetry, firmware, certificates, policy) separate from the video application, sold as a hardware-plus-subscription SKU with a fixed term (he believes three years). Wesco can register as the Cisco distributor and replace the Meraki SKU with a Wesco subscription SKU shipped with the camera. He previously tried to get corporate to create a line of Axis/Meraki-licensed camera part numbers and could not get traction. He also suggested entroCIM could appear as an integration inside the Meraki dashboard. Cisco already bought entroCIM for Cisco Smart Spaces (the Coda building, which Darryl worked on).

**5. Second anchor opportunity: Cisco Connected Real Estate.**
Cisco runs NetBotz-only out-of-band monitoring in MDFs globally: a heartbeat with no vitals and no power data. Growth has outrun its energy measurement. Dave McCain (Cisco Global Account Manager) has a three-part opportunity: a survey (one third), a platform (one third), devices (one third). Bob's illustrative figure: 187 field-deployed NetBotz sites to replace, with no budget identified. The prior internal owner may have been David Chang, who has left; Bob will find out who it moved to and will tell Dave the capability is in development.

**6. Validation standard: three to five anchor customers across verticals.**
Bob recommends three to five named customers in different lanes (he named pharma, auto, retail), drawn from Wesco's top-priority accounts. His litmus test: if it cannot sell to the top customers, no one will pick it up for the rest.

**7. Business sponsor.**
Darryl asked whether Bill Muczko would be the CDA business sponsor (the role Dirk Naylor holds for entroCIM and Brad Stevens for WAM). Bob: yes, Bill "would be happy to review this and be a nominated sponsor." Bill was on the earlier call and is excited about CDA; it aligns with his goal of moving the sales force from pick-pack-ship to advanced-services selling. Bob will remind Bill of that as the value proposition, and asked to be copied on Darryl's note.

**8. Organizational context.**
Bob knows Chris Mooney well and said Mooney's sellers "own column A and column B" of the Axis/Cisco relationship; Wesco is the largest Axis install base and has not capitalized on pairing it with Cisco. Darryl intends to move CDA into Mooney's organization as well. Bob's view on adoption: "Getting our organization to buy on" is the real challenge, not the concept. He also rated the Schneider EcoStruxure integration (in development for entroCIM) as "really huge."
