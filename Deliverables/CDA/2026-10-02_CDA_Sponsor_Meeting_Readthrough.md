## Lead with the business logic, not the technology

- CDA is a distribution play more than a product play. It monetizes the install base, field presence and device relationships Wesco already has.
- There is no new category to believe in. The devices are already in the buildings.
- Bill's own observation from the August review: roughly 85% of his accounts are not managing camera assets effectively, and those that do use spreadsheets. Directional, his estimate; do not quote it as a statistic.

## The commercial model is the strength

- One annual fee per site covering hardware, software and services. Wesco owns the hardware, so the customer commits no capital.
- Site size and a complexity uplift are the only commercial variables.
- It removes the CapEx objection and makes renewal an outcomes conversation.

## Sales enablement is the sharpest argument

- A typical Wesco seller is not a technology seller and is not schooled on what the systems and devices do from a data perspective. CDA does not require that.
- The pitch fits in one sentence: we keep an eye on the devices you already bought.
- No systems architecture, ontology or digital twin conversation is needed to close it. The platform story comes later, with the right people in the room.
- This is the sponsorship argument. It matches Bill's stated goal of moving sellers from pick, pack and ship toward advanced-services selling. He is backing something his people can sell, not a technology bet.

## The wedge strategy

- CDA is the data collector that opens the door to entroCIM, on the same SkySpark and entroCIM foundation.
- State it as deliberate strategy with this internal audience rather than letting it surface as a surprise.
- Customer-facing, CDA still stands on its own and should not read as an entroCIM add-on.
