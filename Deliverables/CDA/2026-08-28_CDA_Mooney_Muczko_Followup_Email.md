Subject: Connected Device Assurance, follow-up and materials

Chris, Bill,

Thanks for the time on [day]. Bill, your point that cameras go unmanaged or sit in spreadsheets across most of your account base is the clearest version of this problem I have heard. The brief is attached, and what I need from you is up front, with the background and the answers to your questions below.

What I'm asking for. This is an eight-month build next year with my existing team, so I'm not asking for a commitment to sell. I am asking for your view that it is worth building, and your help finding two or three pilot accounts at low or no cost with enough scale to test it properly. It's a small first commitment for the customer, one appliance and an agreed scope of devices with nothing for them to staff or adopt, which should make a pilot easy to place.

Next steps:

1. I'll come back to you, Chris, in the next few days to set up the session with Suzanne Betterton and her team.
2. Bill, I'd like to do the same with your senior directors or the GAMs closest to this.
3. From both of you, pilot candidates. Chris's point is the right one: customers should tell us whether this matters to them or whether they are already solving it another way.

Background, and answers to what you asked:

Connected Device Assurance is a Wesco managed service for the connected devices our customers install. We connect once through a Wesco-supplied appliance, inventory and continuously assess the devices, and remediate with the customer's approval. One accountable owner instead of a set of manufacturer tools nobody runs.

Bundled or standalone. Both. There is a large gap in the existing installed base, and it attaches naturally at new construction or a refresh where the customer is buying hundreds of cameras.

Cameras or wider. Cameras first because that is the biggest gap and our connectors are already there, but any device on the network is in scope, including equipment we did not sell.

Deployment. Both. It's hybrid: the cloud side already exists and runs in our Azure environment, so our people deliver the service from tools they use today. On the customer site it's containerized, so it can run on their server, but my recommendation is that we supply it in a box. The first node is sized at 250 cameras or more and we scale from there.

Adding manufacturers. Discovery needs no manufacturer-specific connector, so Bosch, Pelco and the rest are visible from the start. A connector is only required for live telemetry and controlled action. Hanwha and Axis are built and in production today, and a new one runs eight to twelve weeks when we have a device to work against.

entroCIM. Not required. The customer buys the service and never sees the platform behind it.

The brief is internal, so please keep distribution inside Wesco.

Thanks again for the time and the candid input.

Darryl
