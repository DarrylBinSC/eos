# entroCIM Commercialization: v10.1 to v11 Comparison
Built 2026-08-10. Sources compared: `Wesco_entroCIM_Commercialization_2026-07-28_v11.pdf` (50 pages) against `Wesco_entroCIM_Commercialization_2026-07-28_v10 1.pdf` (50 pages); `Wesco_entroCIM_Commercialization_Report_2026-07-28_v11.pdf` (35 pages) against `Wesco_entroCIM_Commercialization_Report_2026-07-28_v10 1.pdf` (36 pages). All four in `Products/entroCIM/`.

Method: full text extraction and line-level diff of both pairs, plus per-slide title inventory of both decks and section/appendix inventory of both reports. Numeric claims counted and compared individually.

## Headline

v11 is a delivery revision, not an analytical one. No figure, score, source, or recommendation changed in either document. What changed is voice, sequence, and where supporting argument sits. The stated rationale, from the report's own version history, is that sections repeating a point made elsewhere were moved to the appendices rather than cut, and that headings and summary copy were rewritten to follow the executive voice guide (lead with the message, active voice, short declarative sentences, no hedging).

Two consequences follow, and they pull in opposite directions. The main argument is materially easier to deliver in a room. The cross-reference apparatus underneath it broke, and the version labelling of both documents is wrong.

- The ask: enablement inside existing budgets, no new headcount, no major capital.
- Zero em dashes in either report version.
- Report appendices A through J all intact and unchanged.

## Smaller items

- The deck's competitive section still reads "Feature content is cut ~30–40% from the prior version." That described an earlier transition and now implies content was cut from v10.1, which is not what happened.

## Assessment

The voice rewrite is the right call for this audience. v10.1 read as an external assessment of Wesco delivered to Wesco. v11 reads as a leader inside the business making a case, which is the correct register for Engel and Khurana and is consistent with the executive voice guide the version history cites.

The appendix relocations are defensible in three of four cases. Deal mechanics, product-marketing functional detail, and third-party research are genuinely supporting material, and moving them raises the signal density of the main flow. A14 is the exception and is treated in the review file.

The failure mode in v11 is not analytical. It is production discipline: two wrong version labels, an overwritten version-history row, and eleven stale cross-references, all introduced by an otherwise sound restructure. On a document going to six named executives, those are the errors that get noticed first and that cost credibility disproportionate to their substance.
