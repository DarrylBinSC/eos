**Produced:** 2026-09-10, revised the same day after the slide-by-slide review. Supersedes the 2026-09-09 version.

**This file does not go to Sean and is not in the deck.** It holds the position, the counterarguments to expect, and the honest risks.

## The position

Pursue the transfer. Digital Products takes the ADM development resources from the Platforms team in India.

The reasoning is control and accountability, supported by a delivery record. Darryl owns the product outcome and does not own the capacity to deliver it. More than a year of development has been slow, quality has been questioned inside his own team, and the pattern in the record is consistent: responsive on small self-contained work, slow on anything needing sustained capacity. That is a control problem before it is anything else, and it does not resolve itself while accountability and capacity sit in different organizations.

## The strongest form of the argument

Lead with schedule, because it is the least arguable. Four major data capabilities were re-estimated in August to land between October 2026 and February 2027, well past the year they were committed in. Then defect burden: 29 defects confirmed still open on review, and a historical backlog parked without a prioritization decision. Then the stabilization plan that has run since July and has not closed. Then the pattern, which is the point: the work that lands is the work that can be scoped and finished alone.

Close on what changes. Backlog control sits with the organization that carries the outcome, prioritization stops being a negotiation across an organizational line, and the three items that are unblocked today get done because someone is accountable for them.

## What to expect back, and the answers

**"What does moving the team actually change? You told me most of this is blocked elsewhere."** This is the strongest counter and Sean may well make it. The honest answer, and it is in the pack: of twelve blockers, three can be closed by a development team today, two are development work gated on decisions held elsewhere, and seven are not development work in any organization. Do not pretend otherwise. Say that the transfer is necessary and not sufficient, that it fixes the half Darryl can be held to, and that the seven are exactly what the air cover request is for. Answering this well is what separates a control argument from a complaint.

**"Is this a skills problem or a management problem?"** Say management and control, and stop there. The record carries one piece of skills evidence, the ADB-skilled data engineer replacing Vishal. Asserting a skills problem on one data point invites a challenge that cannot be won, and it makes the ask look personal.

**"What will it cost me?"** Unknown, and that is the largest single unknown. If the work stays in India the labor cost is broadly unchanged and the change is managerial. Any North America assignment is unbudgeted at a 4.3x differential against a zero North America line. Do not promise it is cost-neutral.

**"What breaks during a transition?"** The September release scope, the Violet Moyer enrichment commitment, and the Strev QA and release governance obligations all run through it. Name them before Sean does.

**"Why should I believe you will do better?"** Because of what is on the commitment side of the ask page. Coverage and completeness figures inside two weeks, the 2027 Strev cost, and a dated complex-build plan. All three are things nobody is blocking today and nobody has done. Offer to be measured on them whether or not the transfer happens.

**"Should we just stop instead?"** The record cannot support a stop decision today, because whether the supply chain programs that earn the revenue depend on ADM specifically or on Strev with manual loading is not established. It is answerable in a week. Offer to answer it rather than argue against the option.

## Honest risks in this ask

The three unblocked items are unblocked and undone, and they are Darryl's. Sean can reasonably ask why a transfer is needed to do work nobody is preventing. Get ahead of it: name them as a gap, commit to dates, and do not let them be discovered.

Akansh Gangil is a documented single point of dependency across requirements, EUDM data quality, the enrichment MVP, complex-build modeling and Strev planning. A transfer does not fix that and could worsen it during transition. Raise it unprompted.

The record does not establish who sets the ADM backlog today, the funding line, or whether the India Platforms team is Merwan Mereby's specifically. If Sean asks a precise organizational question, say it is not established rather than guessing.

The nearest precedent runs the other way. On 2026-08-26 the CMP tool was ruled to stay with the India Platforms team under Merwan Mereby rather than move to Product, with Sean as the counterpart. He may remember. The distinction to draw is that CMP is a tool whose business owner was moving elsewhere, and ADM is a platform whose product accountability already sits in Digital Products.

## What to hold back

Do not put motivation or morale on the page or in the room as an argument. It cannot be evidenced, it reads as a judgement on people rather than on scope and capability, and it is the one line in this that could travel badly if the conversation is repeated.

Do not bring the Strev 2027 funding-attribution question into this meeting unless Sean opens it. Mixing a funding question into a resource question invites the answer that the money waits for the ownership call.

Do not defend the investment to date. Sean's recoverability concern is about forward cost against forward value.
