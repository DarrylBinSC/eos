# Darryl Benson — Executive Communication and Artifact Preferences

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
**Sensitivity:** Internal

## 1. General Style

Darryl prefers communication that is professional, direct, neutral, measured, business-oriented, clear, executive-ready, and concise without eliminating necessary reasoning.

Avoid promotional language, excessive enthusiasm, defensive wording, generic management advice, vague ownership, unsupported certainty, and technical detail without business relevance.

### Naming discipline (added 2026-07-21, from Darryl)

This system (the "EOS" / "Executive Operating System") is Darryl's personal tool, not shared with others. Any deliverable meant to be sent, presented, or shared outside this workspace (emails, Teams messages, slides, memos, briefs handed to someone else) must not refer to "EOS," "Executive Operating System," or otherwise name this tool — those references would not make sense to an outside reader and don't need scrubbing at share-time because they should never be written in the first place. Internal working files (Briefings, living documents 01-10, Products, Dashboard) may continue to reference the EOS by name as before; this rule applies to outbound/shareable content only.

### AI-tell avoidance (added 2026-07-14, from Darryl)

In any text Darryl will send as himself (email, Teams, slide text, docs):

- No em dashes / long dashes. They read as AI-generated and unproofread. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses instead.
- Minimal bold. Overuse of bold emphasis is an AI tell. In prose deliverables, use bold rarely or not at all; structure should come from short paragraphs and lists, not emphasis. (Markdown working files inside the EOS may still use bold for structure; outbound text should not.)

### Do not cast allies as gatekeepers (Darryl, 2026-10-02)

In a CDA update to Nathan Kehr, Darryl removed a line saying Bill's sponsorship "gives us the sponsor you said we needed before moving forward." Nate is an ally and champion; framing his advice as a condition he imposed mischaracterizes the relationship. Before drafting to someone, check whether the record shows them setting a condition or offering counsel, and write to the relationship as it is. ### Slide subtitles and value statements (Darryl, 2026-10-14)

From the Sean portfolio deck, CDA slide. A small-caps line under the title reading "A service powered by technology: Wesco runs it, the customer gets the outcome" was removed. Darryl's reasons, which generalize: it reads as AI-generated; a horizontal list of clauses is an odd form for a subtitle; "powered by technology" is generic; "the customer gets the outcome" is vague because it names no outcome and no reason it matters, so it reads as marketing boilerplate rather than a value statement. The standing rule (no slide subtitles) holds. **Region labels inside a slide are fine** (a small-caps label over a diagram region or a timeline, as on the WAM today and Ascent slides); the distinction is whether the line labels a part of the slide or summarizes the slide. When a removed subtitle holds one real idea, put it where the eye lands after the visual (the takeaway bar), stated concretely: what the customer pays, what they get, who runs it.

### Re-engagement messages: shared agreement, not "you said" (Darryl, 2026-10-14)

When reopening a topic with someone whose earlier input supports the ask, do not quote their words back to them ("you said," "your advice was"). Even when accurate, it reads as holding them to it. State the earlier conclusion as what the group agreed, name the leaders who are aligned now, and keep second-person phrasing to the ask itself. Origin: CDA refresh email to Chris Mooney, v1 to v2.

### Status-check messages (observed 2026-07-15)

When nudging peers/directs for status rather than forcing a decision, Darryl goes lighter than the structured template: one or two sentences, open-ended question, no option lists or deadlines, and framed to leave room for information he may be missing (e.g., an undocumented decision). Example sent: "Ahead of Friday's call, let's talk about Showcase. Are there any unresolved issues or decisions we need to make to make sure JG and Mike are moving?" Reserve the full context/ask/owner/date structure for escalations and decision-forcing messages.

Related pattern (2026-07-15, Zscaler follow-up to Senthil/Amy): when suggesting process improvements to peers' organizations, frame leader-to-leader, not directive. Elements: acknowledge the team's good work first, normalize the issue ("first field deployment, part of the learning curve"), present the ask as a suggestion ("it may be worth..."), and offer help ("happy to have my team help document it"). No deadlines on peers.

**Avoid deferential/submissive framing with peers (corrected 2026-07-16, Anna Baker draft):** Darryl is senior to or a peer of most people he messages, and his own work carries equal weight to theirs. Drop softening language like "know you've got a lot on your plate," "no rush," or "whenever you get a chance" — these read as submissive and understate the priority of Darryl's ask. State the context and the ask directly and briefly. Courteous, not deferential. This does not override the lighter/no-deadline register for genuine status-check nudges (07-15 pattern above) — it applies specifically to cutting apologetic or self-minimizing phrasing.

## 2a. Slide Titles (added 2026-08-28, from Darryl)

**Titles must not sound dramatic.** Where a page carries both a title and a headline beneath it, the two have different jobs and the drama comes from confusing them.

- **The title names the page.** A noun phrase, plus a date qualifier where the sequence matters.
- **The headline says the thing.** It is the sentence.

When the title also tries to be a sentence it has nothing left to say and reaches for effect instead. "From Q4 2027: the loop closes, and the swivel-chair goes away" was a title competing with the sentence directly beneath it; "End state: integrated portfolio, from Q4 2027" is the same page with the drama removed and nothing lost.

**Use the requester's own words for section names.** Where someone has asked for specific items by name, title the pages in their language. Lisa Orton asked for current state, interim state and end state; titling the pages that way makes the deck visibly answer the request, which matters when it is forwarded. Accuracy is a lesser consideration than recognition here: "end state" is imprecise when the roadmap runs three more years beyond it, and it was kept anyway because it is her term.

**Keep dates in the titles** where the deck has a temporal spine, so someone flipping through gets the sequence from the title bar without reading body text.

**Do not put internal working language on a slide.** "The picture in three frames" was our own design vocabulary; nobody in the room has that metaphor.

### Slide titles, second correction (added 2026-09-29, from Darryl)

Titles that try to be descriptive read as AI-written. "Where the model came from, September 18", "The method we followed", "Adjacent markets, one site", "Market size, by site", "What a site costs Wesco", "What we can validate, and what stays an estimate" were all rejected on one deck. The replacements Darryl accepted are plain nouns: "Sales leadership input, September 18", "Method", "Adjacent markets", "Market size", "Cost to serve", "Evidence and estimates".

The rule, tighter than the 08-28 version: **one or two words wherever the page allows it, a date qualifier where the sequence matters, and nothing that reads as a description of the page.** "Pricing model", "Funding path" and "What is needed" were cited as correct length and tone. The headline beneath the title carries the sentence; the title only names the page.

## 3a. Financial and Metric Data (added 2026-07-14, from Darryl)

Never present multiple financial metrics inline in prose — hard to read and comprehend. Use a compact labeled table or KPI chip strip (label above, bold value below), ordered so the layout tells the story (e.g., funnel order: pipeline → created → won → booked → billed), with one takeaway sentence beneath. Match existing deck visual language (e.g., value chips) where applicable.

## 3b. Submitted Form Fields (added 2026-08-17, from Darryl)

Applies to any field written into a form that goes to a reviewer: AOP demand collation templates, intake forms, business cases, governance submissions.

**The reader is the reviewer, not Darryl.** These fields are the deliverable itself, not working notes about the deliverable. Correction given 2026-08-17 on the WAM FY27 Pro-Forma field, which had been drafted in analytical voice and read as thinking out loud.

Rules:

- **No instructions to Darryl.** "Confirm with Tom Boss," "owner Tom Boss," "raise with Barbara Serdeczna," "to be attached before 08-21" do not belong in a submitted field. Those are change-log or register content.
- **No reasoning chains.** State the conclusion. "Which is arithmetically true and not defensible without isolating incremental revenue" is analysis; "the figures shown are total performance rather than incremental return" is the fact a reviewer needs.
- **No meta-commentary about the analysis.** Lines like "this sensitivity rests on the P&L's own structure rather than a separate model" reassure the author, not the reader.
- **Caveats become qualifications, not worries.** State them flatly and briefly ("gross margin is held constant across the range"), not as open anxieties or things to chase.
- **Target roughly 150 words** for a substantive field. Structure: what is being submitted and on what basis, what it produces, what it means for the decision, then qualifications.
- **Lead with the number and its basis**, not the reason a number is difficult to produce.
- **Do not justify the design of the field inside the field** (added 08-17, third correction in one session). Causal framing such as "each reported with its coverage denominator, *because* measurement access differs between deployments" argues for a choice in a space whose only job is to state it. Cut to "reported with stated coverage." State conditions; do not defend them.
- **Prefer specification voice over process voice.** "Baselines are established in Q1 2027" reads as a definition; "Baselines established Q1 2027, first report Q2 2027" reads as a plan still being worked out.
- **Never commit to a metric whose measurement depends on permission Darryl does not hold** (added 08-17, from Darryl). Customer-outcome metrics requiring a customer to agree to share results are the common case. His test: adding a metric later puts you ahead; committing now and failing to report it is a credibility problem in the first reporting cycle. Prefer a set that is entirely self-measurable, then treat anything customer-dependent as upside disclosed verbally.
- **Named customer references need a deliberate decision each time.** A numeric anchor drawn from one account (points licensed, utilization) is usually premature on a submission; the target stands without citing its derivation. A named reference engagement illustrating an outcome is a different case and can stay.

Working analysis still gets written, but it lives in the accompanying change log or decision-log entry, where the audience is Darryl. Keep the two registers separate: the form is executive-ready, the log is candid.

## 4. Slide Content

**Template (added 2026-10-05):** slides go on the Wesco template by default unless Darryl specifies otherwise. Mechanics in `Deck_Construction_Standards.md` section 0.

Darryl generally wants:

- Executive summary rather than dense bullets
- Clear product and commercial separation when useful
- Benefit statement in the primary write-up
- Speaker notes with supporting definition and nuance
- Explicit dependencies
- Clear current state versus future state
- Direct wording
- Limited jargon unless the audience is technical

### CEO deep-dive update slides (Darryl, 2026-10-19, John Engel deep dive)

Learned while updating the entroCIM and WAM update slides in the Engel deep-dive deck (slide owner's template: Product Update / Commercial Update, Business Sponsor and Product Executive line).

- Keep the deck owner's slide structure; update content bullet by bullet from the EOS record rather than rewriting the slide.
- Mirror the original's verb-led openers (Delivered, Established, Scheduled, Reduced); in-progress items take a present participle (Reconciling, Transitioning, Working with).
- Four or five bullets per section at most; one line to a line and a half each. If a bullet needs a second clause to explain itself, it is too detailed for the CEO.
- Internal governance names are welcome (DCOE, AI Council, C2P, FY27 AOP, GPOs); they ground the update in company process. Rollout day counts, feature inventories, per-learner prices, vendor names and reviewer-level detail go to the talk track.
- Realized-value figures belong on the slide when they exist (WAM Sales Operations scorecard); attribution of who reported them stays verbal.
- Drop bullets that describe ordinary delivery work (a data-enrichment release, an order-coverage change); the CEO reads them as noise.
- Constraints are stated as one plain sentence of dependency, not a list of gaps and owners; the detail sits on a backup slide.

### CEO pre-read slides, prospectus register (Darryl and Sean McCloskey, 2026-10-20)

Sean's feedback on the 2026-10-19 Engel deep-dive slides: the deck is a standalone pre-read for the CEO and CIO and should read like a prospectus. This supersedes the presented-deck rules directly above whenever a deck will be read without a presenter.

- There is no talk track. Anything moved off the slide is invisible, and any claim that needed a verbal caveat is stated unqualified. Confirm it or cut it.
- A prospectus describes an asset to someone deciding whether to keep funding it: what it is, who buys it, what it has produced, where it is going. Open with one positioning line. State outcomes and position, not activity.
- Bullets are outcome-led (what the customer or the business now has), not verb-led team activity.
- No internal governance names, version numbers, tool names or rollout mechanics (DCOE, C2P, AI Council, release numbers, day counts). Translate the business point: "meets Wesco's IT security standards," "approved through Wesco's AI governance."
- Commercial Update carries customers, sales figures with an as-of date, and the revenue model. Operating changes (capacity, support model) appear only as the plan they produce.
- Sales figures only, no plan attainment: Sales presents the plan separately through the business sponsor, and there is no upside in the product team presenting it too.
- Named customers are fine on an internal pre-read; omit any account in an open reduction or dispute (Stanford, 2026-10-20).
- Four bullets per section, five at most, one to one and a half lines each.

## 7. Mermaid and Architecture Diagrams

**Diagram and graphics tools (agreed with Darryl, 2026-10-07).**

- **Relationship diagrams (stakeholder maps, decision-rights maps, influence grids): build in Miro** when the Miro connector is available; Mermaid is the fallback. Mermaid stays the standard for diagrams embedded in Markdown or the Word pipeline (briefings, .md headed to `Pandoc/`). Reference build: board "Stakeholder Map 2026-10"; generator `Deliverables/Stakeholder_Map/build_scripts/build_miro_stakeholder_board.py`.
- **Deck graphics: Miro is the sketchpad, PowerPoint is the deliverable.** Workflow: (1) sketch two or three layout options, each in its own 16:9 area (1280 by 720 works, as on "Launch Studio Sean Deck 2026-10"), side by side in one Miro frame; (2) Darryl picks and marks up on the board; (3) rebuild the chosen option natively in PowerPoint on the Wesco template, simplified for the slide; (4) a Miro image goes in a deck only as an appendix or reference diagram nobody else edits.
  - **Rebuild from the board, not from memory (2026-10-15).** Before step 3, Claude reads the chosen option back through the connector. For a hand-built sketch the read-back gives each shape's position, size, text, color and every connector's endpoints (verified on the Launch Studio board), so Darryl's markups carry over and positions scale onto the slide. For a synced Mermaid flowchart the read-back gives the code (nodes, edges, labels, direction) but not positions; Claude lays out native shapes in the same direction and uses Darryl's view of the board as the visual reference. Tested end to end 2026-10-15 for a hand-built sketch: `Deliverables/EOS_Meta/build_scripts/build_slide_from_miro_readback.py` turns a saved read-back into one native slide on the Wesco template (uniform scaling, Miro 15 px text to 10.5 pt, card headings one step up, one shape per connector, endpoint check built in), and the result passed the endpoint check and the render check. Treat its output as the first draft of the slide, then apply the deck's content rules.
  - **Once the deck moves on, the board is stale.** When a deck revises wording or structure after the sketch, the deck is the master for that content. Before rebuilding from a board again, Claude compares the read-back with the live deck and either updates the board first or rebuilds from the deck. Found in the 2026-10-15 test: the Launch Studio board still carried the 2026-10-13 wording ("Inlet filter") that the 2026-10-14 deck had replaced.
  - **Then the deck standards apply in full:** one connector shape per connection, endpoint check, render every page and look at it (`Deck_Construction_Standards.md` §1 to §4). Real names, financials and NDA content go in at the rebuild, never on the personal-account sketch.
  - **Appendix images** come through the Miro export inbox, from a 16:9 frame. A Mermaid render goes in a deck only as engineering backup, never on a main slide: it is off-template and cannot be edited.
- **When to sketch in Miro first:** more than about 8 elements, connectors crossing between groups, the layout itself is the open question, or alternatives are wanted. **Go straight to PowerPoint** for standard patterns (2x2, timeline, 3 to 5 step flow, simple grid, table) or about 8 elements or fewer with no cross-group connectors. Tiebreaker: if the layout can be described in one sentence before building, go straight to PowerPoint. Sketching costs a double build, so use it only when the layout is uncertain.
- **Account and data boundary:** work in Darryl's personal Miro account for now (he also holds a Wesco Miro license; switching means reconnecting the connector and choosing the Wesco team at sign-in). Boards stay private, never shared or given collaborators unless he asks. Wesco policy is strict, so **personal-account sketches use placeholders for customer names, financials and anything under NDA**; real content goes in at the PowerPoint rebuild.

**Integrating Miro and Mermaid (agreed with Darryl, 2026-10-15; `03` 2026-10-15).** The rules above choose the tool by use case. These govern how the two work together.

- **Choose by destination.** Mermaid when the diagram lives in Markdown or goes through `Pandoc/`, changes often, or will be maintained by an LLM (process flows, architecture, register charts). Miro when the layout is the message (relationship maps, influence grids), when Darryl will mark it up by hand, or when options are compared side by side. Deck graphics follow the sketch-then-rebuild workflow above.
- **One declared source of truth per diagram.** A Mermaid diagram's source is its block in the .md or its `.mmd` file. A Miro diagram's source is the board, and every Miro board used for EOS work gets a row in `Reference/Diagram_Register.md` (purpose, what it is the source of truth for, generator and source data, last export, sensitivity, status). Miro is invisible to grep, to other LLMs and to any session without the connector; the register is how they find it.
- **Promote; never maintain both.** When a Mermaid diagram exceeds the limits in `Mermaid_Standards.md` §24, or its layout becomes the open question, use it as structural input to build the Miro board, then mark the Mermaid file `SUPERSEDED_` or label its header "structural draft only, not maintained." Miro back to Mermaid is rare: only when the diagram must live in an LLM-maintained file. Claude reads the board as SVG through the connector, regenerates the Mermaid, and marks the board superseded in the register.
- **Miro into Word.** Export the frame from Miro as an image (PNG if offered; JPG is fine and is what Miro's frame export produced in the 2026-10-15 test). Never SVG in the Word pipeline. Darryl can save it under Miro's default name; Claude renames it. Word scales the image to page width, so what decides legibility is the frame, not the export: frame tightly around the content (the export includes all empty frame area), one layout per frame for a document, and a frame no wider than about twice its height. In the test, a 2900 by 1700 frame holding two options side by side exported at 9142 by 5359 pixels (1.3 MB) and its body text was too small to read at page width. Save beside the deliverable as `<deliverable>_diagrams/<name>_YYYY-MM-DD.<png|jpg>`; the date in the name is the staleness check, because board edits never flow into an export. Update the register's last-export date. Path and caption rules are under "Markdown for the Word pipeline."
- **Data boundary, extended.** In the personal Miro account, the placeholder rule also covers internal stakeholder alignment and influence ratings and anything else Darryl has marked private, not only customer names, financials and NDA material. Where real values must appear, use Mermaid (rendered locally on Darryl's Mac by mermaid-filter) or the Wesco Miro license.
- **Synced Mermaid diagrams in Miro, for flowcharts (tested 2026-10-15; `03` 2026-10-15).** Miro's Mermaid diagram widget keeps the code and the visual diagram in sync both ways, and the connector can create the widget from code and read the current code back. For flowcharts (process flows, architecture, data flows, handoffs), this replaces the "promote; never maintain both" rule above:
  - **One code, two homes.** The Mermaid code is the source of truth. It lives in the EOS .md (with the HTML comment naming board and widget id) and in the Miro widget. The board gets a row in `Reference/Diagram_Register.md`.
  - **Build.** Claude writes the Mermaid in the .md and creates the widget from the same code.
  - **Edit on either side.** Darryl edits visually in Miro or in its code panel; Claude edits code.
  - **Sync before use.** Before changing the .md or sending any document that carries the diagram, Claude reads the code back from the widget. If the board changed, the board's code replaces the .md block, because the board is where Darryl edits. When Claude changes the code, it updates the widget in the same session.
  - **Never switch the widget to free-form.** It breaks the link to the code permanently, and the diagram falls back under the hand-built board rules.
  - **No PNG export.** The watcher renders the same code into Word. The Word render will not match Miro's layout exactly, so Claude checks the rendered image before the document goes out.
  - **Spacing for Word.** Use Regular or Wide spacing in Miro for any diagram headed to Word. In the 2026-10-15 test, Narrow spacing (nodeSpacing 20, rankSpacing 25) made group titles collide with boxes in the Word render.
  - **Colors.** Wesco colors set in code survive. Colors picked in Miro's visual editor come from Miro's palette; Claude restores Wesco colors in code if the diagram leaves the team.
  - **Syntax.** Mermaid version 10. Subgraph ids without hyphens. Edge labels plain text. The frontmatter `config:` block that Miro writes renders correctly in the watcher.
  - **Scope.** Flowcharts only. Sequence, class and ER diagrams render from code but are not yet visually editable in Miro; treat them as code-only until tested. Relationship maps, grids and anything placed by hand stay as hand-built boards.
- **Portability.** ChatGPT, Copilot and other tools cannot see Miro. A transfer package carries the Mermaid source, or the exported PNG plus two lines on what the diagram shows and where its source of truth lives.

## 11. Executive Writing Craft (added 2026-07-20, distilled from `Engel_Executive_Voice_Guide.md`)

Apply to any executive communication of a complicated subject (summaries, memos, exec updates, slide narratives):

1. **Identify the format first** (slides/bullets, team message, exec memo, email/quick note), then write to that format.
2. **Lead with the message.** State what is true and what matters most first; never bury the headline under context or process.
3. **Cut, don't pad.** Remove execution language, hedging stacks, and filler. Test: if a word can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.
4. **Placeholder discipline.** Never invent metrics, names, dates, or outcomes; use clearly marked placeholders like `[metric]` or `[date]`.
5. **Preserve decision-changing content when shortening:** what is ready, what is in progress, dependencies, risks, owners, timing.
6. **Avoid-list:** hype absolutes ("world-class," "game-changer," "best-in-class," "synergies," "leverage" as a catch-all verb), jargon and tool names that don't carry the business point, long compound sentences.
7. **Length discipline:** shortest version that fully carries the message; slide bullets one line where possible, never past two.
8. **Close substantive deliverables with a one-sentence executive takeaway** when it adds clarity.
9. **Flag editorial choices** (cut sections, merged points, missing facts) under a `---` line so Darryl can review.
10. **Label processing mode up front** (added 2026-07-20, from Darryl's Sean/ADM message): in any multi-item executive message, open by telling the reader how to process each item — awareness only vs. decision needed vs. action requested — so attention lands before reading (e.g., "Three items. The first is awareness only; the second two need a decision with you."). Per-item leads like "No need to cover this on our call, but I want you aware of..." carry the same signal inline.

Note: the full Engel guide in `Reference/Engel_Executive_Voice_Guide.md` is an audience-calibration aid for Engel-bound or CEO-register material only. Darryl's own default voice remains as defined in this file (including §1 AI-tell rules, which override any conflicting formatting habits in that guide, e.g. em-dash use).

## Tell them only what they need (2026-09-29)

Darryl, reviewing the CDA AOP one-pager: "Only tell him what he needs to know, not what he doesn't." The draft had named a decision to hold an account out of the plan; the reader did not know that account was ever in, so disclosing the removal created a question he did not have.

The test for an executive brief is not "is this true and interesting" but "does this change what the reader decides or says." Working detail that passes the first test and fails the second belongs in the product file or a deck appendix, not on the page. This applies to internal decisions already made, methodology behind a number, structures the reader has not been introduced to (account counts, tier schemes, phasing), and open items the author owns rather than the reader.

## Briefings: write for scanning (2026-10-19)

Briefs are read in the web app's Briefs tab, usually between meetings. Darryl, 2026-10-19: the reader was "not as easy to read as I'd hoped." A readability scan of that day's weekly showed why: vocabulary was fine (grade 10.8), but 21 of 32 text blocks ran past 80 words. The rules:

- A paragraph or bullet stays at or under 60 words; a sentence at or under 35. One idea per bullet.
- Each Top-3 priority keeps its labelled fields (Business impact, Why you personally, Risk if delayed, Next step, Expected outcome). The reader turns them into a card, one field per line, with the next step highlighted; Word shows them as written.
- Coverage and counts go in a short bulleted list, not a run-on sentence.
- "Changes since last brief" is its own section.
- Check with `Tools/brief_readability.py` before saving.

## Markdown for the Word pipeline (2026-10-05)

Applies to any .md that goes through `Pandoc/` to become a .docx. The template and filter are described in `Pandoc/README.md`; these are the writing rules that make them work.

- **One title line, then sections.** Open with a single `# Title`. Use `##` for sections and `###` below that. The filter turns the opening `#` into the document title, which also fills the footer. Use more than one `#` only in long documents whose top level is genuinely parts, such as a deep dive; the footer still carries the title.
- **No file paths or backticks in anything that leaves the team.** Paths such as `Products/CDA/CDA.md` render in gray monospace and mean nothing to Sean, a customer or a partner. Name the document in words or leave the reference out. Internal EOS files and briefings may keep them.
- **Callouts, sparingly, always with a bold label.** Four named styles, written as a fenced div:

  ```
  ::: {custom-style="Decision Required"}
  **Decision required:** approve the enterprise pricing construct by 2026-10-15.
  :::
  ```

  `Bottom Line` (top and bottom rule; the one-sentence takeaway, at most one per document or major section), `Decision Required` (shaded box; the ask, the owner and the date), `Risk` (dark left bar; a risk the reader must act on, not every risk), `Source Note` (small gray text; evidence basis or data source under a table or chart). The bold label carries the meaning, so the document still reads correctly in grayscale or to a screen reader. A plain `>` blockquote is a neutral note (gray box) and is fine for status banners.
- **Every diagram gets a caption.** Put an `Image Caption` div immediately after each Mermaid block. The caption doubles as the image's alt text, and for charts it names each series, since Mermaid cannot draw a legend.
- **Embedding an exported image (Miro export, slide image) (2026-10-15).** Write the path from the EOS root: `![](Deliverables/CDA/<name>_diagrams/<file>.png)`. The older `../Deliverables/...` form also still works. A path that does not resolve does not fail the conversion: Pandoc replaces the image with its description, the watcher prints a warning, and the warning is kept in `Pandoc/output/<name>.log`. When Claude feeds the pipeline, it checks for that log before copying the .docx. Follow every image with an `Image Caption` div, as for Mermaid.
- **Marking where a Miro diagram goes (2026-10-15).** The image line is the placement marker, and it goes in the .md at the exact point the diagram should appear. Put an HTML comment on the line above it naming the board and frame; Pandoc drops the comment, so it never shows in Word, but it tells any later reader or LLM where the picture comes from. Write the line with its planned dated file name before the export exists. Until the PNG is saved, the Word draft shows the caption in place of the picture and the watcher warns about the missing file, which is the reminder to export it. Verified 2026-10-15.

  ```
  <!-- miro: board "Launch Studio Sean Deck 2026-10", frame "Slide 1 options: What Launch Studio is" -->
  ![](Deliverables/Vibe_Code_Productization/<deliverable>_diagrams/launch_studio_path_2026-10-15.png)

  ::: {custom-style="Image Caption"}
  Launch Studio: from business prototype to supported product.
  :::
  ```
- **Who exports from Miro (2026-10-15).** Darryl, from Miro (frame, Export, PNG). The connector reads a board only as Miro's own structural SVG (shapes, text and connector references, not a drawn picture), so Claude cannot produce a faithful image from it. Claude can read that SVG to check or regenerate content, not to export.
- **Miro export inbox (2026-10-15).** Miro has no API for exporting a frame as an image (Miro developer forum, staff answers 2021 to 2024; still open January 2026), so the export stays a manual step, and everything after it is Claude's:
  1. Claude places the image line and the board/frame comment in the .md and tells Darryl which frame to export.
  2. Darryl exports that frame into `Pandoc/miro_exports/`, keeping Miro's default name ("<Board> - <Frame>.jpg"; a colon in a frame name becomes an underscore).
  3. Claude matches the file to the comment by board and frame name, renames it `<name>_YYYY-MM-DD.<ext>`, moves it into `<deliverable>_diagrams/`, fixes the extension in the image line if needed, runs the watcher, checks the render, and updates the export date in `Reference/Diagram_Register.md`. The inbox is left empty.
  - Frames headed anywhere should be named for what they show (the name becomes the file name) and built export-ready: tight around the content, one layout per frame, page-shaped for documents and 16:9 for slides.
  - Not automated through the browser: driving Miro's export UI would be slow, fragile and approval-heavy, and the download would land outside the connected folders. Revisit if Miro adds an export API.

- **Tables: five columns or fewer where possible.** Column widths size themselves to the content. To size columns deliberately, make the dashes in the divider row unequal (`|--|------------|----|`) and the filter leaves the table alone.
- **Long documents only: `toc: true`.** Add YAML front matter with `toc: true` for documents over roughly ten pages. Never on daily or weekly briefings. Word asks to update fields when the file opens; answer yes to populate the contents.


## Numbers and error codes in outbound incident emails (2026-10-07)

From the Strev outage awareness email to Amy Aguirre, Senthil Govindaswamy and Natalie Marquardt.

- **No figure goes out unless its source and basis are known.** A 279 failed-transaction count came from a Copilot summary of a Teams chat, did not match the API CoE's figure, and could not be reconciled. Darryl's call: remove it rather than send a number nobody can stand behind.
- **Write error codes so they cannot read as counts.** "500 timeout and 401 token-expired failures" reads as 500 of one and 401 of the other to a non-IT audience. Describe the failure in plain language and put the code in parentheses after it: "timing out (HTTP 500)".
- **Report what a vendor said, not what it implies.** Strev named an SSO and token-generation outage without tying it to either error; "Strev reported an outage," not "Strev confirmed the cause."

## Plans for a deliverable (2026-10-07)

Darryl, on the first data center value proposition plan: "This may be a bit over thought." The first draft carried seven workstreams, a working hypothesis, a red-team workstream, a nine-slide outline and a risk section. The revision he accepted had five content elements in his own words, segment priority, who to align with, inputs and a short schedule. For a plan to build a deck or brief: state the purpose in his words, what the deliverable covers, who must be aligned before building, inputs and dates. Leave out hypotheses, challenge workstreams and risk sections unless he asks. Alignment with the sales lead (Nate for entroCIM) comes before drafting, not after.
