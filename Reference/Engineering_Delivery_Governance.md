# Product, Engineering, Delivery, and Governance

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
**Sensitivity:** Internal

## 1. Scope

Darryl's organization spans the lifecycle from strategy through operations:

- Product direction
- Roadmap
- Backlog
- Architecture
- Software development
- Release
- Deployment
- Support
- Managed Services
- Customer escalation
- Cybersecurity
- Vendor and partner integration
- Benefits realization

## 2. Operating Model

A healthy model distinguishes product ownership, engineering ownership, architecture authority, delivery ownership, deployment ownership, support ownership, service ownership, commercial ownership, customer-success ownership, and executive risk acceptance.

Unclear boundaries create escalation load and duplicated work.

## 3. Governance Principles

- Decisions need an owner, forum, and date.
- Recurring meetings should produce decisions or measurable progress.
- Daily execution belongs with managers and team leads.
- Executive forums should focus on tradeoffs, risk, funding, ownership, and outcomes.
- Temporary workarounds need retirement plans.
- Release readiness should include deployment and support.
- Architecture decisions should include business and delivery consequences.
- External vendors require explicit accountability and escalation paths.

## 4. Hybrid Agile Context

Darryl has used or evaluated hybrid Agile approaches.

A pragmatic model should combine product outcomes and roadmap, backlog discipline, sprint-level execution, release governance, cross-team dependency management, customer commitments, architecture and security gates, and executive decision checkpoints.

Avoid process purity that obscures delivery or business value.

## 5. Release Management

A release review should include outcome and scope, customer impact, completed and deferred work, testing, defects, security, architecture, deployment, training, communications, support, rollback, risk acceptance, and benefits or leading indicators.

## 6. Customer Escalations

Evaluate customer issues based on revenue impact, strategic importance, delivery risk, relationship risk, product or support implication, repeatability, escalation level, and whether executive involvement materially changes the outcome.

Darryl should direct accountability and resolution rather than personally troubleshoot.

## 7. Cybersecurity and Risk

Known work has included API-key exposure, vendor remediation, risk registration, Docker Hub and artifact-management governance, security review, SSO, and architecture and DevSecOps processes.

The executive role is to ensure ownership, severity, containment, remediation date, validation, communication, repeatability, and vendor accountability.

## 8. Vendor Management

For external platform or delivery dependencies, require:

- Named business owner
- Named technical owner
- Contract or SOW relevance
- Impact
- Severity
- Workaround
- Root-cause path
- Committed date
- Escalation trigger
- Executive counterpart

## 9. Delegation Standard

Darryl should generally own strategy, executive alignment, cross-functional decisions, high-impact risk, organization and operating model, investment, customer executive escalation, and portfolio communication.

Directors and team leaders should own execution, backlogs, defects, team coordination, deployment tasks, detailed project plans, routine status, and technical investigation.
