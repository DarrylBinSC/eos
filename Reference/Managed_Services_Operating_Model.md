# CSS Managed Services — Operating Model

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
**Sensitivity:** Internal / Confidential

## 1. Executive Orientation

CSS Managed Services is intended to become a scalable, repeatable operating capability across facility operations, asset management, safety, remote monitoring, and site operations.

It should be treated as an operating model, not merely as a feature attached to a software product.

## 2. Strategic Objective

Create repeatable service delivery that combines digital platforms, enterprise data, customer workflows, monitoring and analytics, human expertise, support, customer-success practices, commercial ownership, and continuous improvement.

## 3. Initial and Expanding Scope

Historical planning positioned Intelligent Facility Services, supported by entroCIM, as an initial technically dependent service area.

The broader model is intended to expand across:

- Intelligent Asset Management
- Intelligent Safety
- Remote Monitoring
- Site Operations
- Cross-platform service delivery

## 4. Ascent-Led Service Delivery Framing

For some integrated opportunities, the model has been framed as:

> Ascent-led service delivery, powered by CSS Platforms.

In that model:

- Ascent provides the customer-facing service wrapper, governance, reporting, and workflow coordination
- WAM / Strev provides asset-lifecycle and renewal visibility
- ADM provides normalization and enrichment
- entroCIM provides OT integration, telemetry, alarms, and analytics
- Connected Safety / Spanr provides safety workflows
- Managed Services and local partners perform verification, onboarding, tagging, commissioning capture, document loading, training, support, and operational execution

This is a useful solution pattern, not necessarily the universal organization model.

## 5. Operating-Model Questions

Every service needs clear answers to:

- Who owns the service?
- Who owns product capability?
- Who owns delivery?
- Who owns commercial execution?
- Who owns customer success?
- Who owns support?
- What roles are required?
- What is standardized?
- What remains customer-specific?
- Which platform and data capabilities are required?
- How are costs allocated?
- How is quality measured?
- What benefits can be demonstrated?

## 6. Phased Development

### Phase I — Establish the Initial Service

- Define ownership
- Establish initial roles
- Standardize core delivery
- Focus on Intelligent Facility Services
- Prove the operating model

Historical resource concepts included a Portfolio / Product Manager and Building Optimization Lead. Role names and staffing status must be validated.

### Phase II — Expand Across Service Lines

- Apply the model to asset, safety, monitoring, and site operations
- Build reusable processes
- Clarify customer-success and commercial ownership
- Establish scalable staffing and partner models

### Phase III — Integrate and Automate Across Managed Services

- Use lessons from live service operation
- Improve automation
- Integrate platforms and workflows
- Improve support and observability
- Optimize SLAs
- Reduce manual effort
- Improve margin and scalability

## 7. Benefits

- Recurring service revenue
- More consistent customer outcomes
- Faster onboarding
- Standardized ownership
- Repeatable delivery
- Better use of platforms and data
- Improved customer retention
- Stronger cross-sell opportunities
- More scalable support
- Better margin through automation

## 8. Failure Modes

- Treating Managed Services as a sales label without an operating model
- Unclear service owner
- Product teams absorbing service work
- Customer-specific delivery without reusable standards
- No customer-success ownership
- No cost or margin visibility
- Automation introduced before the process is understood
- Platform features presented as service outcomes
- Staffing added without defined accountabilities

## 9. Executive Evaluation

A Managed Services review should include the offer, target customer, service owner, delivery owner, commercial owner, customer-success owner, roles and capacity, standard work, technology, data, SLA, cost, price, margin, benefit, current customers, pipeline, risks, and next decision.
