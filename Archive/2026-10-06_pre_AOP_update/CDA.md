## Market adjacency and device-class coverage (added 2026-09-20, Darryl direct)

**Darryl's positioning, 2026-09-20, and it changes the sequencing:**

- **Cameras first because of Wesco's concentration in that business, not because CDA stops there.** It is the logical starting point for an unsophisticated Wesco seller: familiar product, existing install base, a call point the seller already has.

## Commercial model correction, 2026-09-29 (Darryl direct)

- **The offer is one annual fee per site covering hardware, software and services.** Multi-year commitments are handled later as a discount, not as a different pricing structure. The three-year term in the model is now an internal cost-recovery period for upfront cost, not a customer commitment.
- **Defect Darryl found and v2.6 fixed: upfront software cost was being dropped.** The bill of materials had an upfront column, but the per-site build pulled only hardware and commissioning into upfront cost, so licence purchase and activation never reached the price, the payback or the capital figure. Site upfront is now hardware plus software upfront plus commissioning; capital is hardware plus software.
- **Software renewals now escalate** at their own input rate, separate from people-cost escalation, and the BOM carries renewal cycle and uplift columns that suggest the rate.
- **entroCIM and SkySpark licensing is priced by deployment size (v2.7, Darryl 2026-09-29):** a 1,000-point deployment costs less than a 5,000-point one, so the licence is not a flat per-node cost. The BOM carries a four-row block (Small, Medium, Large, Complex) with upfront and annual licence cells that feed the tier software lines directly; the entroCIM row is excluded from the per-node totals so nothing is counted twice. Open question on the block: whether the licence basis is points, devices or nodes, which decides whether Complex costs more than Large.
- **Software maintenance and renewal set at 18 percent of the upfront licence cost (v2.8, Darryl 2026-09-29),** the usual maintenance rate on a licence purchase. A switch on the Inputs tab selects between the 18 percent rule and annual prices quoted directly by a vendor, so a quoted subscription and a maintenance percentage are never counted together. Consequence while the BOM is unsourced: the annual software placeholders are now inactive, and the upfront licence placeholders are too low to imply a sensible maintenance figure, which understates the fee until real licence prices are entered.
- **Pricing posture, Darryl 2026-09-29 (Durable): the fee is a published price Wesco sets and holds, not a cost-plus calculation that moves with component costs.** The customer never sees the component costs and judges value on the total service price, so cost changes move Wesco's margin, not the customer's price. Full price by default; discount only where a multi-year commitment or a negotiation requires it. v2.9 implements this: a published fee per tier, a cost-plus reference fee used to set it, realized margin and the gap to target shown per tier, and a switch to derive the fee while a new tier is being priced.
- **entroCIM licensing follows the quartermaster model (v3.1, Darryl 2026-09-29, supersedes the fixed-increment reading in v3.0).** Wesco buys a 50,000-point SkySpark licence at $59,773 and subdivides it down to 1,000-point deployments, so the cost is points drawn times $1.19546 per point: 1,000 points is $1,195.46, 2,500 points is $2,988.65. entroCIM PODs are costed separately at $1,000 per 1,000 points. Site tiers return to Small 1,000 points, Medium 2,500, Large 5,000, and Complex a Large deployment plus extra hardware. Maintenance at 18 percent applies to purchased software (the licence, Nozomi, RMM, Airwall) and not to the POD build, which the product team maintains.
- **Model structure decisions, Darryl 2026-09-29 (v3.2).** Hardware is not refreshed at the end of the recovery period: devices are monitored and replaced as needed, or at an agreed technology refresh, typically 5 to 7 years, so no refresh falls inside the five-year plan. No churn is modelled, because the model's purpose is the business case that justifies the development. Sales cost is now carried: commission at 3 percent of the annual fee for a site's first three years, and marketing at $50,000 a year as a portfolio cost. Gross margin now appears per account on the Accounts tab and on a new Account P&L tab; the product team sits below the gross-margin line and is allocated there for reference only, by sites or by revenue on a switch.
- **Tiers are kit sizes, not per-site engineering (Darryl, 2026-09-29, Durable).** A site takes the size tier that fits it, and the customer conversation is size and value, never points. Costing at the tier size is therefore correct: that is the licence and hardware actually provisioned. **Complex is a difficulty-of-connection adder, not a fourth size** (v3.3): a complex site takes its size tier plus an extra collector, extra commissioning and service hours, and a fee uplift. Cisco's MDFs are the case that proved it: 500 points, small in size, hard to connect.
- **Model audit, 2026-09-30 (v3.3 fixes).** Hardware now amortizes over its refresh life and software over the recovery period. Separate hardware and licence cost escalators. Implied price per device is computed per account from actual devices. The per-camera fee moved below the gross-margin line so it can be removed cleanly if the team drops it. Sector size mixes re-set for the current tier sizes, with a separate complex share per sector.
- **Consequence to watch:** with the fee flat and software escalating, 2031 gross margin drifts from 40% to 39%. Either index the annual fee or hold the escalation at zero, a decision for the pricing conversation.
