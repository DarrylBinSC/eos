## How This File Works

Four registers. The delineation is the point.

| Register | Contains | Test | Briefing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. My Actions** | Darryl personally takes the next step | If Darryl does nothing, this does not move | Daily, rule-selected, max 7 |
| **B. Accountable, Delegated** | Darryl's org owns it; named executor; Darryl engages at a gate | Answerable for the outcome, not for the next step | Weekly, condensed |
| **C. Awaiting Others** | Owed to Darryl | Darryl's action is chasing, not doing | Weekly, condensed |
| **D. Standing Responsibilities** | Recurring role duties, no completion state | Cannot be closed | Not tracked here. See `07_Operating_Cadence.md` |

**Attribution test (strict).** An item enters Register A only if: (1) evidence shows Darryl stating he will do it, (2) Darryl confirms it, or (3) it is direct follow-through on a decision Darryl owns with no other executor named. Items raised in meetings Darryl attended do **not** qualify automatically. Copilot attributions labeled "Inferred" never create a Register A item; they are surfaced once as a confirmation question.

**Dating rule.** Every Register A item carries a date or a named review trigger. Nothing stays undated. Sorted by date.

**Focus rule.** The daily briefing surfaces at most 7 Register A items: due within 14 days, or on a Key Product Due Dates critical path, or aging 14+ days with no movement.

**Closure discipline.** No item closes on Copilot evidence alone. Mark `Potentially closed — confirmation required` and surface at the weekly closure check.

**Answered-elsewhere test (added 2026-08-26).** The closure check asks whether an item has been *answered* before it asks whether it has been *done*. A register item is a question with an action attached, and the question can be answered by another route entirely: a submission completing, an ownership decision landing, the thing being built under a different name. When that happens the action is moot even though nobody performed it, and the item will otherwise sit indefinitely because nobody is failing to act on it. On 2026-08-26, five of the nine items closed in one sweep were of exactly this kind, and every one of them had been visible in the daily briefing for weeks. Run this test first.

**Forced disposition at 14 days (added 2026-08-26).** Register A items do not age out automatically, because Darryl is the actor and silence is not evidence of completion. But surfacing an item is not deciding it: the Focus rule above already showed these items daily, and they still did not close, because re-reading an item and ruling on it are different acts. So once a Register A item passes **14 days beyond its date with no movement**, it stops being surfaced as a task and is surfaced as a three-way question: **done**, **re-date with a reason**, or **kill**. There is no fourth option and no silent carry. The reason on a re-date is mandatory, because "still open" without one is precisely how the backlog state was reached.
